Plan A. Primary route

Field Notes: The Farm Data Project

Est. window: 1.5–2 weeks on site
within Aug 5–28

A passion project rooted in family legacy, carrying a real impact layer alongside it. Built to flex around however much time actually ends up spent on the farm.

Before arrival

Scope and Brainstorm

  • Ask what questions matter to the family and the farm itself
  • Weigh candidate themes: soil, crop health, livestock, irrigation, yield
  • Narrow to one or two focal themes ahead of time
On site

Collection

  • Photos and video, phone or drone if available
  • Sensor logs or manual field notes
  • GPS-tagged field walks
  • Short interviews with family
After collection

Structure and Clean

  • Organize by field and by date
  • Label imagery: crop type, health, pests
  • Build a light schema, CSV or JSON
Back in the city

Analysis and Model

  • Trend charts across time
  • Basic crop or health classification if imagery is rich enough
  • Otherwise, solid descriptive statistics
↓
Framing

Narrative Layer

  • Family legacy framing throughout
  • Before and after, season over season
  • Impact angle: sustainability, small-farm resilience
Outcome

Portfolio Deliverable

  • Interactive dashboard or story-driven web app
  • Visual reports: maps, charts, photo timelines
  • Short write-up on process and why it matters
Weather advisory: feasibility checkpoints
  1. 1.5 weeks or more, rich data: run the full pipeline, imagery and classification included
  2. Short window, sparse data: scale down to the narrative and descriptive version, still Plan A, just lighter
  3. Collection falls through entirely: access, weather, or timing blocks it, pivot fully to Plan B
Reassessed live once actual farm dates are locked in, before Aug 28. Either version keeps the personal and impact framing, not just a technical exercise.
Margin note. Open thread Which single theme makes the strongest personal story? One told well beats several told thinly. Worth checking what family records or past yield data already exist before arriving, so the on-site window goes toward what is genuinely missing.

Plan B. Backup route

L.R. TactiCam

Sports CV / sequel to the World Cup app

Tracks soccer players and the field itself to generate tactical overlays. A direct follow-up to the World Cup app, built to activate quickly if the farm plan cannot run.

1

Concept and Scope

  • Which tactical questions: formations, pressing, spacing, heatmaps
  • Reuse the World Cup app's visual language and branding
2

Source Footage

  • Broadcast clips or public match footage
  • Fixed tactical-cam angle, ideal for homography
  • Start with a handful of sample clips
3

Detect and Track

  • Player and ball detection with a pretrained CV model
  • Multi-object tracking with persistent IDs
  • Team classification by jersey color
4

Field Registration

  • Detect pitch lines and corners
  • Homography, warp to a top-down tactical view
  • Map player coordinates onto a standard pitch
↓
5

Tactical Overlays

  • Formation shapes
  • Heatmaps and coverage zones
  • Pressing triggers, spacing lines
  • Speed and distance metrics
6

Portfolio Deliverable

  • Interactive replay app, scrub through a match
  • Exportable tactical clips
  • Sequel framing tying it back to the World Cup app
Locker room note. Open thread

As a sequel, the World Cup app's existing design system and reusable code, video ingestion, UI shell, could shortcut the build. Worth deciding early whether tactical overlays should be static images or a scrubbable video player, since that choice shapes the whole pipeline.

Both plans share the same six-stage shape for portfolio consistency. Decision reassessed live during the Aug 5–28 trip.
